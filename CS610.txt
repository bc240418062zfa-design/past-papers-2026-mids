According to the sources, there are four primary types of delay: Ans ● Propagation Delay: This is the time required for a bit of data to travel across the physical medium (like a wire or fiber optic cable) from one point to another. ● Switching Delay: This is the time a network component (such as a hub, bridge, or packet switch) takes to process and forward data to the next part of its journey. ● Access Delay: This is the time a computer must wait to gain control of the medium before it can begin transmission (for example, waiting for a token in a Token Ring or using CSMA/CD in Ethernet). ● Queuing Delay: This occurs inside packet switches when a packet must wait in a line (queue) because the switch is busy forwarding other packets that arrived earlier. How to identify them in a scenario: ● If the scenario mentions the distance or speed of the cable/medium, it is Propagation Delay. ● If the scenario involves a device processing or moving a packet from one port to another, it is Switching Delay. ● If the scenario describes a computer waiting for its turn to speak on a shared network, it is Access Delay. ● If the scenario describes packets backed up in a buffer or waiting for a busy link to clear, it is Queuing Delay.
