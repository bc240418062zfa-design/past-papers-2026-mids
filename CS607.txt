Detailed Explanation: Genetic Algorithms (GAs) are search and optimization techniques inspired directly by Charles Darwin's theory of biological evolution. In nature, organisms that are best suited to their environment survive and pass on their traits to the next generation. GAs apply this exact concept to solve complex computational problems using the following mapped steps: Population (Organisms): Instead of animals or plants, the algorithm starts with a "population" of random, potential mathematical or logical solutions to a problem. Fitness Function (Survival): Every solution is evaluated and given a "fitness score" based on how well it solves the problem. Selection (Survival of the Fittest): The algorithm selects the solutions with the highest fitness scores to become "parents". Crossover (Reproduction): The selected parent solutions are combined to create "offspring" solutions. Mutation (Genetic Variation): Random, small changes are occasionally introduced into the offspring to ensure diversity.
